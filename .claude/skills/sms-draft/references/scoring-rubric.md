# Scoring Rubric — picking the one to ride

> This **ranks the variants that already PASSED the QA checklist** — it does not re-gate. It's a proxy for
> the one question that matters: *which of these is the ICP most likely to reply to?* The score orders them;
> **the strategist makes the final pick, and the market decides the real winner.**
>
> Derived from what the best winners share. Score each variant 0-100; higher = more likely to land.

## The dimensions (weighted)

| # | Dimension | Weight | What earns the top of the band |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Relevance** (tiered) | **30** | Feels written *for them* |
| 2 | **Sounds human** | 25 | Reads like a person thumbed it out |
| 3 | **Proof fit** | 20 | The outcome is exactly what they want, in their words |
| 4 | **USP / mechanism intrigue** | 15 | Makes them want the "how" |
| 5 | **CTA (soft + relevant)** | 10 | Easy to say yes to, and *about them* |

### 1 · Relevance — 30 (the heaviest, on purpose)
- **26–30** — Clay-specific / speaks their *exact* world (their role, their niche signal, their stack;
  "B2B SaaS spending $5k+/mo on Google Ads but not ranking"). Feels hand-written for this one person.
- **16–25** — speaks their world *generally* (right niche language, but not personalised to them).
- **0–15** — generic; would read the same to anyone. (A passing-but-generic variant still loses here.)

### 2 · Sounds human — 25
- **21–25** — free-flowing, manually-typed, lands like a real text; zero AI tells.
- **11–20** — mostly human, a couple of stiff/markety spots.
- **0–10** — reads like AI or a marketing line.

### 3 · Proof fit — 20
Not raw strength (the case study is already vetted) — **how exactly the outcome matches what *this* buyer
wants, said in their language**, plus the strength of the case.
- **16–20** — the outcome is the *specific* growth they want, in their world ("more commercial roofing
  jobs" to a residential roofer eyeing commercial; "contested probate billables" to an estate lawyer) — and
  the case is strong.
- **8–15** — a strong, believable result, but not the *specific* thing they want, or not in their words.
- **0–7** — thin/vague, or an outcome they don't actually care about.

### 4 · USP / mechanism intrigue — 15
- **12–15** — a genuinely intriguing mechanism that opens a "how?" loop.
- **6–11** — a mechanism that's fine but not a hook.
- **0–5** — none, or forced.
- **N/A** — if the mechanism is *correctly omitted* (SEO/commodity), don't penalise it: score this dimension
  neutral and rank on the other 85. **Never reward an invented mechanism** — a made-up "how" scores 0, not high.

### 5 · CTA (soft + carries relevance) — 10
- **8–10** — soft *and* tied to an idea/strategy/audit for them ("had a few ideas for {{company}} — chat?").
- **4–7** — soft but generic ("worth exploring?" with no hook).
- **0–3** — hard, or a bare "can we chat / give me a ring" with no relevance.

## Output
Rank the passing variants by total, name the **top pick + a one-line why**, and surface the runner-up so the
strategist can choose. **Tie-break order: sounds more human → more relevant (written for them) → mechanism
sounds better.** ("Human over clever.") The skill ranks; it never marks the final winner or sends.
