## Step 4 — MECHANISM MENU → **RECOMMENDATION: OMIT.**

This is the narrow-but-clear **omit** case, and the playbook is explicit that *this exact client* is the cautionary tale.

**Why omit:**
1. The honest mechanism is "we ranked them." That just **restates the result** — there's zero curiosity in it (`mechanism-development.md`, omit-condition #1). "by ranking them #1" isn't a separate idea from "#1 on Google" — it's the same idea twice.
2. `pattern-library.md` names Big Leap directly: forcing a DTC-seeding-style unique mechanism onto an SEO offer is *why it scored 5/10.* Don't re-make that mistake.
3. Manufacturing a fake "unique mechanism" (some named "GEO framework" / "AI-signal methodology") is the precise failure mode that tanks SEO offers. I will not hand you one.

**So instead of a mechanism, spend those words on the things that actually carry an SEO text:**

| Instead of a mechanism, use... | Why | Drawn from |
|---|---|---|
| **Specificity — the actual keyword** ("#1 for [their money keyword]") via Clay | this is the real differentiator in every winning SEO text | W5, W9, W14 |
| **The "without ads / without paid" wrapper** ("$400k in pipeline, without spending a dollar on ads") | compresses the marketing leader's real pain — burning budget on paid to hit pipeline | W9, W10, the wrapper section |
| **The AI-search angle as the "mechanism-shaped" bite** ("ranked #1 on Google *and* in AI search") | this is the closest thing to a real differentiator you have — it's not a mechanism, it's a *scope* of the result, and it's fresh | your raw result; no winner has it yet |

> Important distinction so we're honest: **"ranked them #1 in AI search too" is not a mechanism** — it's *part of the result* (where they now rank). That's exactly how to use it: as proof scope, not as a manufactured method. We are NOT claiming a secret AI-ranking technique; we're claiming the outcome (they show up in AI answers).

**The one connector-led line I'd allow if you insist on a mechanism slot** (plain-English, the only honest option): **"by ranking them #1 for their buyer's keywords"** — but note it's redundant with the proof, and W9 only gets away with it because the {keyword} variable makes it specific. If you're not Clay-ing the keyword, drop the slot entirely.

## Step 5 — WAYS TO COMBINE (the dots — you connect them)

One mechanism explains one result at one scale; since we're omitting the mechanism, the "combine" job is **which proof framing pairs with which T2 carrier** (specificity / without-wrapper / authority stack). Clean pairings:

- **A — The default, like-for-like with W9/W8:** **P1** (#1 + $400k pipeline / 6mo) → T2 carries **relevance** ("for [their keyword/category] the same way") + soft CTA. Safe, proven, no mechanism. Closest to a known winner.
- **B — The differentiation play (my lean):** **P4 or P5** (#1 on Google *and* AI search) → T2 carries the **AI-visibility pain** ("everyone's losing clicks to AI answers — got you ranked *inside* them"). This is the freshest, most "aimed" version for a 2026 SaaS marketing leader, and it's the one thing no winner in the file has. Mechanism still omitted.
- **C — The pain-compression play:** **P2** ($400k pipeline, just from organic) **+ without-wrapper** ("without spending on ads") → T2 carries **authority stack** (other SaaS logos you've ranked, à la W8's Daniel Defense / 4 Patriots). Good if paid-spend fatigue is the confirmed top pain.
- **D — The scale play (only if defensible):** **P3** (~$800k/yr run-rate) → for a marketing leader who thinks in annual pipeline targets. Use only if you're comfortable defending the annualization on a call.

Do **not** pair P3 (the chunked-up $800k/yr) with a hyper-specific single keyword — scale framing + one keyword is a slight mismatch. Big number → broader "their category's high-intent terms"; specific keyword → the concrete $400k/6mo.

## Step 6 — OPEN QUESTIONS (your calls — let's go back and forth)

1. **Pipeline vs revenue vs closed-won.** You said "$400k in pipeline." Can you defend it as **revenue/closed** on a call, or is pipeline the honest ceiling? Pipeline is a fine native unit for a marketing leader — but if some of that pipeline *closed*, "$X in new revenue" or "$X in closed deals from organic" hits harder. Which is true?
2. **How aggressive to chunk?** Is the ~$800k/yr run-rate (P3) defensible — was the $400k linear across the 6 months, or front/back-loaded? If it ramped (SEO usually compounds), the run-rate is *conservative* and P3 is safe-to-strong. Your call on how hard to push it.
3. **Can we Clay the actual keyword?** P5 is the strongest relevance play but needs each prospect's real high-intent keyword in a variable. Do you have/ can you build that, or are we stuck at generic "high-intent keywords"? This materially changes which framing leads.
4. **How real / nameable is the AI-search win?** For P4/P5 — are they genuinely surfacing in ChatGPT/Perplexity/Google AI Overviews for those terms, such that a closer could say it plainly and defend it? If yes, I'd lean you hard toward combine **B** — it's your one true differentiator. If it's softer than "they show up in AI answers," pull back to the Google-only P1.
5. **Is paid-spend fatigue the confirmed top pain?** That decides whether the "without ads" wrapper (combine C) earns its place or just adds noise. Check Layer A.
6. **Is Big Leap a nameable/recognizable client + can you name the SaaS client?** A recognizable B2B SaaS logo does heavy lifting (W2/W8 logic). If the client is a name a marketing leader would know, naming it strengthens every framing; if not, the keyword-specificity + AI-search angle carry instead.
