# Case-study developer — Kynship / Supergut

**Case study (as given):** Supergut paused $100k/mo of Meta spend, then hit **$1m/mo in new customer revenue in 3 months**. Mechanism: seeded **~1,200 unpaid micro creators** and ran **their content as the paid ads**.
**Persona:** Founder / CEO, DTC health & wellness.
**Offer/niche:** Paid Meta / UGC / creator seeding for DTC health-wellness brands.

A quick note before the menus: this is one of the rare cases where **the mechanism carries** (per the pattern library it's the bite in ~3 of 17 winners, and the two Supergut winners — W16/W17 — are two of them). So I've kept the mechanism central and given you plain pictures, not jargon labels. The proof menu is rich too, because "new customer revenue" is already a gift — it's the founder's native unit *and* it's a sharper claim than topline.

## Grounding — the winners that map to this

- **W16 (Kynship, mechanism-forward):** *"Took Supergut from $100k to $1m/month in 3 months using over 1200 unpaid micro creators' content as creatives on meta."* — leads on the before→after, mechanism concrete and forward.
- **W17 (Kynship, authority/social-proof):** *"Took Supergut from $100k to $1m/month in new customer revenue in 3 months using our viral micro creator seeding strategy. Did this for Grüns & Create Wellness too."* — leads on new-customer-revenue specificity + household-name stack, mechanism deliberately broadened.
- **W1 (Chamber / Transparent Labs):** same niche (DTC supplements/health-wellness), mechanism-forward + contrarian. Confirms: in this niche a genuinely-distinctive mechanism creates real curiosity.
- **W11 (Pirawna / Cymbiotika):** same niche, "$200k to $1.5m/year… using targeted ads to redirect competitors' traffic" — plain-English mechanism rides behind a clean before→after.

**Read:** this niche (ecom founders, the most-pitched buyer) rewards hard authority — specific revenue + a number + a recognizable name — *and* a mechanism that's actually unusual. "1,200 unpaid creators running as your ads" is unusual. Lean in.

## PROOF MENU — ways to say the result

Every framing below is defensible on a call (it's a reframe of the real numbers, never an invention). I've flagged the move and who each fits.

| # | Framing | Move used | Fits / why |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | **"from $100k to $1m/month in 3 months"** | before→after, single timeframe | The strongest shape — a 10x transformation is dramatic, so the delta does the work (proof-framing move #7). The W16/W17 spine. |
| P2 | **"to $1m/month in new customer revenue in 3 months"** | native unit (ecom) + drop the baseline | "New customer revenue" is the sharpest currency for a DTC founder — it screams *this isn't returning-customer noise, it's growth*. Cleaner than the before→after when you want one punchy number. (W17's choice.) |
| P3 | **"added ~$900k/mo in new revenue in 3 months"** | chunk down to the delta | Says the *gain* as one figure rather than the start/end pair. Use if "1m/month" alone feels less concrete than the jump itself. |
| P4 | **"to a $12m/yr run-rate in 3 months"** | chunk up (×12 annualized) | The scale framing — $1m/mo *is* a $12m/yr pace. Defensible as a run-rate. Use if you want the number to feel bigger; flag how aggressive you want to be (see Open Q's). |
| P5 | **"10x'd their monthly revenue in 3 months — to $1m/mo"** | recast the transformation as a multiple | "10x" is vivid and founder-native, and it's honest ($100k→$1m). Watch the Voice Profile: a clean "10x" reads fine on a call; avoid stacking it with other multiples. |
| P6 | **"hit $1m/mo in new customers after pausing $100k/mo in Meta spend"** | keep the pause as the hook | The pause is a *latent without-X* — see the wrapper note below. Founders feel "scaled while spending less," which is the dream. |

**Notes on the proof side:**
- **The pause is gold and underused.** "Paused $100k/mo in Meta and *still* hit $1m/mo" is the buyer's literal fantasy (more new revenue, less paid spend). It can live as a `without`-style wrapper (W7/W9/W10 pattern): *"…without pouring more into Meta"* or *"…after pausing $100k/mo in paid."* Imply, don't over-assert (Voice Profile: "unpaid creators" > "without ever paying a creator").
- **"New customer revenue" beats raw topline here** — it's the strongest *true* currency for this persona and it pre-empts the "but was that just repeat buyers?" doubt.
- Don't stack P1 + P4 + P5 in one line — pick one shape. Multiple number-frames in one breath reads like a dashboard.

## MECHANISM MENU — plain pictures of the tactic (this is the bite — keep it central)

The literal tactic: gifted free product to ~1,200 micro creators (no payment), then ran *their* posts as Supergut's paid Meta ads. Two distinct, sticky ideas live in here:
1. **the scale** — 1,200 creators, all unpaid;
2. **the move** — their organic content *became* the paid ads (real people, not studio creative).

All options are connector-led, as the patterns require.

| # | Mechanism line | Pattern | What it leans on |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | **"using 1,200 unpaid creators' content as their meta ads"** | Plain-English picture | The concrete number + the unpaid angle. W16's exact move — the "1,200 unpaid" is the curiosity spike. |
| M2 | **"by gifting free product to ~1,200 creators and running their videos as the ads"** | Plain-English picture | The fullest plain picture (Voice Profile's canonical example shape: "gifting free product to creators and running ads off their posts"). You can *see* it. |
| M3 | **"by turning real customers' content into their paid ads instead of studio creative"** | Contrarian | Inverts the category default (brands pay for polished agency creative). The bite is "real people, not produced ads." |
| M4 | **"mostly by running ~1,200 micro creators' posts as the ads — no creators paid"** | Plain-English + honest attribution | "mostly by" softens to partial attribution (you ran the engine, not the whole account); "no creators paid" is the latent without-X. |
| M5 | **"using their micro creator seeding engine"** *(broadened label)* | High-level abstraction | W17's deliberate move — when the concrete version won't fit the char budget, fly higher on a label. Only licensed because the proof (Supergut, 10x) is strong. Use the *picture* (M1–M3) if you have the room; this is the fallback. |
| M6 | **"by scaling on unpaid creator content instead of more ad spend"** | Plain-English + pairs the pause | Ties the mechanism directly to the paused-spend hook — the *how* of "more revenue, less Meta spend." |

**Mechanism notes:**
- **Do not omit here.** This is the exception to the omit-rule in reverse: the mechanism is genuinely unusual and the buyer wants it. Omitting would waste the single most differentiating asset.
- **Picture > label.** Prefer M1–M3 (you can see them) over M5 ("seeding engine" / "viral micro creator seeding strategy" = a jargon label). Only drop to M5 if the char budget forces it — and only because the strong proof licenses the abstraction.
- **The "1,200 + unpaid" pairing is the curiosity core.** A round-ish big number of *free* creators is what makes a founder think "wait, how?" Keep both halves if you can.
- Avoid absolutes ("never paid a single creator," "every ad was UGC") — flag to defend-or-cut. "Unpaid" implies it cleanly.

## WAYS TO COMBINE — which proof × mechanism hang together

The scales match across the board (a 1,200-creator engine plausibly *explains* a 10x — no scale mismatch), so most pairings are valid. The interesting choices:

- **Mechanism-forward / curiosity lever (≈ W16):** **P1 (before→after) + M1 or M2.** The 10x jump, then "1,200 unpaid creators as the ads." Leads on the transformation, lands the curiosity on the mechanism. The cleanest, most-proven shape.
- **Authority / social-proof lever (≈ W17):** **P2 (new customer revenue) + M5**, then a logo stack in T2 (Grüns, Create Wellness). Sharper number, broadened mechanism, household names carry. Use when you want the names doing the proving and the mechanism kept tight.
- **Contrarian lever:** **P2 or P3 + M3** ("real customers' content as the ads, not studio creative"). The bite is the inversion; pairs with the cleanest single number so the line stays short.
- **The pause / without-X play:** **P6 (or P1) + M6**, with "after pausing $100k/mo in Meta" as the wrapper. Strongest on the *dream* axis — "scaled while spending less." This is the one I'd most want to test that the existing two Supergut winners *don't* already cover.

One mechanism per result — don't pair, say, M2's full picture *and* M3's contrarian frame in the same line. Pick the lever, pick one of each.

## OPEN QUESTIONS — your calls

1. **How aggressive on the number?** P4 chunks $1m/mo up to a "$12m/yr run-rate." Defensible, but is the run-rate solid enough for *this* claim to survive a call, or do you prefer the conservative, concrete P1/P2? (Founders chunk up — but only if it holds.)
2. **Before→after vs single number?** P1 ("$100k → $1m/mo") shows the journey; P2 ("$1m/mo in new customer revenue") is one sharp punch. Which reads stronger to you for this list?
3. **Do we want to surface the pause?** It's the most distinctive *dream* angle ("more new revenue, less Meta spend") and the existing Supergut winners don't lead on it — worth a fresh test? Or does adding it crowd the line and dilute the mechanism?
4. **Mechanism altitude — concrete or broadened?** M1/M2 (the 1,200-unpaid picture) is the curiosity bite, but it eats characters. M5 (broadened label) is W17's compromise. Which do you want to lead with — and is the logo stack (Grüns/Create Wellness) going in T2 to back it?
5. **"1,200" exact or "~1,200 / over 1,200"?** W16 used "over 1200." Do you have a firm count, or do we keep it soft? (Non-round reads real; soft protects you if the number's approximate.)
