# Mechanism development — mining the bite and sharpening it

The mechanism is **what the agency actually did** to produce the result — and it's the **bite**: the
thing that makes the result believable *and* makes the reader want to learn more. It is a **core pillar**
of the line.

The bite is almost never sitting ready-made in the paperwork. Agency owners often don't fully know their
own IP — they file the real magic under "higher-level stuff" and write something generic on the case
sheet. So the job is not to *lift* a mechanism, and it's not to *invent* one. It's to **mine** the true
detail, **extract** the bite, and **sharpen** it into a line with a hook — staying grounded in something
they really did.

Two ways it reads wrong, and we catch both:
- **Jargon** — "cross-channel MER optimization." Means nothing, sounds automated.
- **Fake-plain** — "running their Meta and Amazon as one pot." *Sounds* simple but says nothing concrete,
  and points to no real detail. This is worse than jargon — it hides the gap. ("Entertaining" points to
  *humor, on their website*. "One pot" points to nothing. That's the difference.)

---

## Step 1 — Mine widely (the case sheet is often the thinnest source)

Read everything and hunt for the **one true detail with a spark** — not a ready-made line:

1. **The master / case-study sheet** — read it, but expect it to be generic ("micro content and paid media
   strategy… when the right strategy is in place"). Usually the *least* useful for the bite.
2. **The website / case-study page** — where the real nuance often hides ("larger-than-life visuals and
   **humor**… unskippable"). This is where Transparent Labs' actual bite lived.
3. **The founder quote / the actual ads / creative** — the texture the paperwork leaves out.
4. **A similar-service winner** in `winners.csv` — how the same kind of work read when it landed.

You're not looking for a finished mechanism. You're looking for the **detail that's interesting** —
the thing that, once sharpened, would make the ICP lean in.

---

## Step 2 — Extract the bite

Pick the one true nuance worth building on, and drop the generic. For Transparent Labs that's **the
humor / unskippable creative**, not "micro content + the right strategy." The test: of everything you
found, which detail would make *this* buyer think "huh, tell me more"?

If the only honest detail is generic process-speak with no spark, the mechanism may be one to **omit**
(see below) — don't dress up "the right strategy" as a bite.

---

## Step 3 — Sharpen the angle

This is the generative half, and it's a **core move, not a fallback.** Take the true detail and find the
sharper, still-true angle by running it against what the category assumes:

1. **What did they literally do?** (funny, larger-than-life video ads)
2. **What does the ICP already assume** about how this result usually gets made? (supplement ads = boring
   ingredient/science lectures you skip)
3. **Where do those collide?** That gap is the hook. (they made supplement ads people *actually watch*)
4. **Say it plain** → "using humor to make their ads **actually entertaining**."

Note what happened there: you did **not** copy their literal words ("make complex science digestible").
You found the sharper true angle. That's the craft — sharpening, not transcribing, and not inventing.

Strip any jargon into a **plain picture** the ICP sees instantly ("cross-channel MER optimization" →
"restructuring their ads around the purchases the algorithm actually rewards"). A short sticky **label**
is allowed only when **strong proof licenses it** (famous brand + big transformation can carry a named
method); ordinary proof needs the plain picture.

Ship every option **connector-led**, never a bare fragment:
- **"by ___"** — plain, direct
- **"mostly by ___" / "mainly by ___"** — softens, implies *partial* attribution (when claiming 100%
  would overclaim — you ran the seeding engine, not the whole account)
- **"using ___"** — clean, neutral (named methods, sharpened angles)

---

## Step 4 — Run every option through the QA gate

A line ships only if it passes all four:

1. **Does it make sense?** Is it plainly **relevant to the actual result** — does it plausibly explain how
   *that* number happened? (entertaining ads → more watched → more sales → a believable 13x)
2. **Is it clear?** Can you tell **exactly what it is**? If the reader couldn't say back what you did,
   it's fake-plain — rewrite concretely or cut. ("one pot" dies here.)
3. **Does it spark curiosity — a little bite, a little hook?** Picture the **real ICP** (the named persona
   — a supplement founder, a Jeremy Reed). Would *they* want to learn more?
4. **Is it realistic / anchored?** Believable and defensible-on-a-call. A line needs **one real anchor** —
   and there are **three valid kinds**: (a) a real **mechanism detail**, (b) a real **Layer A pain/objection**
   the framing dissolves, or (c) a real **status fact** (press, #1, record). Only a line anchored to *nothing*
   real — an invented mechanism or number — is killed. **A bolder *implicit framing* of a real thing is
   allowed** (see "Infer the bolder angle"), flagged "defend on a call." Imply rather than over-assert
   ("unpaid creators" > "without ever paying a single creator"); flag absolutes (ever/single/entire).
5. **Plain over clever.** One tight phrase in **winner tonality** (W1 "to make their ads actually
   entertaining"; W16 "1200 unpaid micro creators' content as creatives"). **Kill cringe metaphors** — "live
   sales floor that rambles", "aesthetic salesforce", "an army selling it live" all read empty. If you
   reached for a clever image, you over-rotated; say the plain thing.
6. **Psyche + no new objection.** Pressure-test against the persona's *ego/fear* — does it make a founder feel
   *replaced* or uneasy ("so the brand didn't have to" reads like you're sidelining them)? And does the line
   itself *spark* a new objection ("selling live = a bit cheap")? If so, reframe.

A line that's clear and true but creates no pull is a weak mechanism — say so honestly rather than
inflating it.

---

## The objection spine (usually the highest-value move — when there's a wall worth dissolving)

For a sophisticated buyer the mechanism that lands is often one that **quietly dissolves a wall** they
already have. Whether it applies depends on the buyer — but for most considered B2B/DTC offers there's a
real wall, so check every time:

1. **Pull the walls from Layer A** — both the **objections** *and* the **pain points** (a pain like "I've
   been burned by slow agencies" is a wall too). **Surface the candidates and recommend the 2-3 that fit;
   the strategist picks which** — it's unrealistic for the skill to fully decide the direction.
2. **Weave each implicitly** — never state the objection; bolt a short *"without ___" / "while ___"* tail
   onto the real mechanism so the wall falls without a word. *(Across offers: SEO + 'agencies are slow' →
   "…ranking them in weeks, not months"; DTC + 'discount-race fear' → "…while keeping their margins"; local
   + 'sketchy lead-gen' → "…with jobs that actually showed up.")*
3. **Label which wall each line dissolves, and show the assembled sentence** — the strategist wants to *see*
   "[outcome] + [mechanism] → dissolves [wall]" in full, not a bare fragment.
4. **Position against what they're burned by** — name the thing the buyer is *tired of* (from Layer A) and
   frame the mechanism as the antidote: "…with [the real move] **instead of [the thing they've given up on]**."
   A single line can quietly neutralise **two** walls (lead dissolves one, the tail another).

## Go heavy on the implicit — say it without saying it

The move that takes a line from 7 to 9 is **implicit**: dissolve the wall through what the line *implies*,
never stating it ("…without [X]" makes them feel safe from X without naming it). When there's a wall, lead
with the implicit version. This is the craft to over-index on.

**Separate from this — inference liberty (allowed, but flagged).** A strategist will also say something the
source doesn't spell out *when it's a fair read of a real detail* (a margin inferred from a healthy AOV; an
incremental lift inferred from a total-revenue number; attribution — "they got there because of us" — even if
the brand was already moving). Offer a couple of these, **flagged "defend on a call,"** only when a real
anchor sits under them. This is NOT fabricating a mechanism or number (still banned) — it's a defensible
inference. Don't conflate the two.

**Full-story variant — only when the ammo's there.** If the case genuinely has a *chain* worth showing
(e.g. a funnel/halo that lifts other channels), offer one variant that shows the chain, not just the single
tactic — it can be the strongest. But many cases are one clean move with no chain. **Never manufacture a
funnel/halo that isn't in the case** — that's the overfit trap (it's a GoFish-shaped move, not a universal).

## Volume, then cut — and branch the service lines you actually have

- **Generate generously — as many per pattern as the ammo honestly supports** (often ~10 when the case is
  rich, fewer when it's thin — don't pad a thin case to hit a number), then **self-cut** the weak/cringe ones.
  The bar for what you keep is **near-final, pick-and-choose quality** — not rough drafts.
- **Branch off the service lines the case actually has.** Chunk the real IP into its components first and
  generate mechanisms off *each* slice — the slices differ by offer (a creator offer: short-form · live ·
  event drops · retargeting; an SEO offer: rankings · content · technical · links). Naming the real slices
  gives the strategist branch-points; don't impose a slice the case doesn't have.

## What good looks like (exemplars, not a taxonomy)

Don't sort the mechanism into a "pattern." These are just shapes that have worked — pull the closest
winner from `winners.csv` by offer/niche to see how it actually read:

- **Just say the tactic plainly** — when it's obviously desirable, plain *is* the bite (W4/5/8/9/10/11/14).
  Especially SEO / commoditized work.
- **Name the position or method** — when a **strong case** carries it high (W2 "the go-to voice for AI
  defense", W16/17 "viral micro-creator seeding").
- **Name the boring default and break it** — when the work genuinely rejects something the buyer
  recognises (W1 humor-led ads → "actually entertaining"). This is the Transparent Labs move. Don't force
  it onto a normal tactic.
- **The timing / the trigger** — when the value is *when* you act, not what you do (W3, W13).

Fuller notes in `sms-playbook/pattern-library.md` — but treat them as "here's what landed," not boxes.

## When to OMIT (the narrow exception)

Drop the mechanism slot only when:
1. **Commoditized / SEO / organic offer** where the honest mechanism just restates the result ("we ranked
   them #1") — no curiosity left. Lean the proof on result + specificity instead.
2. **The case is so strong + specific it carries alone** (famous brand, outlier number).

Even a commoditized offer can usually be made to **feel** differentiated through specificity (the exact
niche, the signal, a "without paid ads" angle) — reach for omit only when mining genuinely turns up no
real detail with a spark. If you omit, **say so and why** — never fill the slot with a manufactured
"unique mechanism" (the exact failure that tanks SEO offers). Omission is the exception, never the default.

---

## Tie the mechanism to the proof

The mechanism should plausibly **explain** the result it's paired with — roughly the same scale and story.
It needn't be a rigid one-to-one (a couple of moves can jointly explain one big outcome), but don't pair a
small tactic with a giant number and call it the cause. When you hand over the menu, note which mechanism
options sit naturally with which proof framings.
