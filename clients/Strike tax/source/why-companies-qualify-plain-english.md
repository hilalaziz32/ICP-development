# R&D Tax Credit — Plain English (the "why do they qualify" explainer)

A non-technical reference for writing copy, handling replies, and pre-qualifying. Grounded in the
striketax.com eligibility page + onboarding. Not legal advice — Strike confirms actual eligibility.

---

## What it actually is (one line)
The government gives you **cash back for money you already spent figuring things out** — mostly the **wages** of the technical people who design, build, or improve your products, your software, or how you make them. It's a reward for doing that work **in the US.**

## The mental unlock (this is the whole game)
Most owners hear "R&D" and picture **lab coats and inventing something brand-new**, so they say *"we don't do R&D."* **Wrong bar.** The legal bar is much lower. If your team:
- **builds or improves a product** (even a customer's custom order),
- **improves how you make it** (new tooling, less scrap, faster line, new material), or
- **writes/develops software,**
…and at the start you **weren't 100% sure how to do it** and had to **try, test, and tweak** — that's qualifying. It doesn't need to be new to the world, just **new to you**, and **it doesn't need to have worked** (failed attempts still count).

## The 4 simple questions (the "4-part test," de-jargoned)
1. **Were you trying to make/improve a product, process, or software?** (better, faster, stronger, cheaper, more reliable — not just looks.)
2. **Was it technical?** (based on engineering/science/software — not marketing or styling.)
3. **Were you unsure how to pull it off at the start?** (some real technical question to solve.)
4. **Did you try different ways to figure it out?** (designed, prototyped, tested, iterated.)
Yes to all four → the wages for that work qualify.

## Where the money comes from
Federal credit ≈ **6–8% of the qualifying spend**, plus a **state credit** on top (40 states; can add a lot). The spend is mostly **technical payroll** — so the credit **scales with how many engineers/developers/designers/chemists you employ in the US.** ~10+ technical staff is where it gets meaningful (often **$200k–$700k+**, sometimes millions).

## Why so many haven't claimed (your reply scenarios, explained)
- **"We don't do R&D"** — the #1 myth (see unlock above). Most common.
- **Their CPA never raised it** — general CPAs aren't R&D specialists; it's a niche study, so it gets skipped.
- **"We're too small / not profitable"** — startups can offset **payroll taxes** even with no profit; size isn't the bar.
- **They didn't know you can go back** — you can amend **up to 3 prior years** for a cash refund.
- **They already claimed** — but often **underclaimed** (missed state credits, didn't max the wages). That's the second-opinion opening.

## Why right now (the campaign hook)
A 2025 tax-law change (OBBBA) reopened a **one-time window** for smaller companies (**≤$31M revenue**) to go back and claim **2023 & 2024** for a cash refund — and that window **closes July 6, 2026.** (2022's window already closed earlier in 2026.)

## For OUR niche — manufacturing / materials (go deeper here)
Manufacturers are one of the **strongest, most-overlooked** fits because the day-to-day work *is* the qualifying activity:
- **Designing/developing parts or products** — CAD, prototypes, custom-to-spec client work.
- **Improving the product** — stronger/lighter/cheaper/more durable; new features.
- **Improving the process** — new tooling/molds/dies, automation, reducing scrap/defects, faster cycle times, throughput.
- **Materials work** — trying new materials, blends, coatings, formulations; testing them.
- **Tooling & mold design**, fixture design, first-article testing during development.
Rough scale (Strike's own): manufacturing averages **~28% of expenses** qualifying; one automotive case had **62% of wages** qualify → **$657k**. The credit rides on **process/design engineers, tooling, and product-dev wages.**

What does NOT qualify: routine production, routine QC after the product's finalized, pure styling/cosmetic changes, and anything done **outside the US.**

**The lesson:** "does lots of R&D" ≠ "fits this campaign." The deadline-refund pitch needs a company that **made profit and paid tax in 2023–24** (so there's something to refund). A funded pre-profit startup does R&D but has no past tax to claw back — different pitch, different list.
