# Pattern Library (two-tier) — derived from the 17 tagged winners

> Built FROM `winners.csv`, not invented. The big lesson in your own data: **what carries a cold text
> is usually the case study + speaking their exact world — NOT a clever mechanism.** The mechanism is
> genuinely the bite in maybe 3 of 17. So the library is two tiers, and you pick Tier 1 first.

---

## TIER 1 — What carries the text (decide this FIRST)

For a chosen case study + offer, decide what does the heavy lifting. Most often it is **not** a clever
mechanism. Pick the carrier, then only reach into Tier 2 if the carrier is the mechanism itself.

| Carrier | What it is | Winners | When it's the carrier |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Case study / result** | named brand + specific number + timeframe | W2, W4, W5, W8, W9, W10, W11, W14 | the default — a strong, believable result said plainly |
| **Specificity / relevance** | speaking their exact world; often Clay-driven (their niche language, the ICP signal) | W3, W6, W13, W14 | you can be specific about *them* / prove you know exactly who they are |
| **Authority** | credibility byproduct — ex-Google, name-drops, case-study stacks. **Not a lever, not a pattern.** Rides inside the proof or mechanism | W7, W8, W15, W16/17 | a credibility marker is the real reason they'd respond |
| **Guarantee** | risk-reversal — remove the risk entirely | W12 | low-sophistication buyer + you can guarantee an outcome |

**The rule (from FUNDAMENTALS):** build the bar that's short. If the result already screams "this is
real," spend the text on **specificity/relevance**, not more proof. Don't stack proof onto a standing bar.

---

## TIER 2 — Mechanism patterns (only when the mechanism carries)

Reach here **only** when the mechanism itself is the bite (rare — W1, W3, W16, partially W13). Otherwise
**omit the mechanism** and lean on Tier 1. Each pattern ships **connector-led** (the connector is part of
the output, never the bare fragment).

### 1. Plain-English tactic — *the dominant pattern*
- **Move:** just say what you did, plainly, when the tactic is obviously desirable.
- **Connector:** "by ___" / "mainly by ___"
- **Winners:** W4, W5, W8, W9, W10, W11, W14 (it absorbs most "named" tactics — incl. the old "competitor-redirect", W11)
- **Fits:** the tactic is self-evidently good (ranking #1 on Google) — especially **SEO / commoditized offers**.
- **Doesn't fit:** the tactic needs explaining → then it's not SMS-ready (don't dress it up as something fancier).

### 2. High-level abstraction
- **Move:** skip the tactics, name the *position* or the *named method*.
- **Connector:** "mostly by ___" / "using ___"
- **Winners:** W2 ("the go-to voice for AI defense"), W13 ("AI-timed outreach"), W16/W17 ("viral micro creator seeding strategy")
- **Fits:** a strong/famous case study (let it fly high), OR the concrete mechanism is too messy to fit in an SMS (W17).
- **Doesn't fit:** an ordinary case study that needs concreteness to be believable.

### 3. Contrarian
- **Move:** name the boring default the whole category does, invert it; frame the inversion as the bite.
- **Connector:** "using ___"
- **Winners:** W1 ("humor to make their ads actually entertaining")
- **Fits:** the mechanism genuinely rejects a default the buyer recognises themselves doing.
- **Doesn't fit:** when it isn't actually weird — don't force the contrarian frame onto a normal tactic.

### 4. Trigger / Timing  *(situational)*
- **Move:** not *what* we do — *when*. Anchor to a precise buyer-natural moment or signal.
- **Connector:** verb-led ("we run ads at the exact moment…") / "using ___"
- **Winners:** W3 (water damage), W13 (the ICP signal: "recently hired a CMO with no agency of record")
- **Fits:** the value comes from catching a discrete moment/signal, nameable in a few words.
- **Doesn't fit:** steady-state offer with no real trigger.

### 5. Risk-reversal (guarantee)  *(situational)*
- **Move:** remove the risk — "X or you don't pay".
- **Winners:** W12 (vet, "10 new appointments or you don't pay")
- **Fits:** low-sophistication buyer where the guarantee is the whole pitch.
- **Doesn't fit:** high-ticket / sophisticated buyers, where a guarantee can read cheap.

## The "without X" wrapper — a frame, NOT a pattern

`[result] without [the thing they assume is required]` — bolts onto a result (+ optional mechanism).

- **Winners:** W7 ("without increasing ad spend"), W9 ("without spending on ads"), W10 ("without referrals or cold calling")
- **Use only when** you genuinely didn't do X **and** the buyer assumes X is mandatory.
- **Imply, don't over-assert:** "unpaid creators" > "without ever paying a single creator". Flag absolutes (ever/single/entire) to defend-or-cut.

---

## ⭐ The omit-the-mechanism rule (the Big Leap fix)

For **SEO / GEO / organic / commoditized offers**, the "mechanism" IS the result ("ranking #1 on
Google"). **Do not force a unique mechanism.** Lead with **Tier 1** (result + specificity), optionally a
**"without ads" wrapper**. **W9 (Velox / Dr Axe) is the exact template.** Forcing a DTC-seeding-style
mechanism onto an SEO offer is precisely why Big Leap scored 5/10.

> Quick test: if the only honest mechanism line is the result restated ("we ranked them #1"), **omit the
> mechanism slot** and spend those words on specificity/relevance instead.

---

## Connectors — the framing carrier (never dropped)

The connector is part of the output. It sets the altitude and the honesty:

| Connector | Feel | Use with |
|---|---|---|
| **"using ___"** | clean, neutral | Contrarian, named methods, Trigger |
| **"mostly by ___" / "mainly by ___"** | softens, implies *partial* attribution (honest — we didn't do 100%) | High-level abstraction; any time full attribution would be an overclaim |
| **"by ___"** | plain, direct | Plain-English tactics |
| **"___ without ___"** | the wrapper | bolt onto result + mechanism |

## How to use this (the wordsmith reads this top-to-bottom)

1. Read `offer-matrix.md` → get the offer archetype, sophistication, case strength.
2. **Tier 1:** pick what carries. If it's *not* the mechanism → omit the mechanism, lean on result +
   specificity (+ wrapper). Most offers stop here.
3. **Tier 2:** only if the mechanism carries → pick the pattern, write it **connector-led**, ≤ the SMS
   budget, in the buyer's own words.
4. Run every line through `tone-of-voice.md`.
5. Score on believability · relevance · differentiation · curiosity · length · traceability.
