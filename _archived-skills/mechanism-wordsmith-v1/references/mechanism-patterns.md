# Mechanism Reframing Pattern Library

Source: extracted from Scaletopia's Winning SMS Templates Database (13 templates). Every pattern below is anchored to at least one verbatim winning example. Patterns NOT in the winning library are NOT in this file — that is the discipline. If a strategist wants to introduce a new pattern, it must be backed by a verified winning template.

---

## How to use this file

For each literal mechanism input, walk through the 8 patterns in order. For each pattern, ask the applicability question. If the answer is yes, the pattern APPLIES and you write one variant for it. If the answer is "kind of", the pattern MAYBE applies — write a variant if it produces something legitimately different from your APPLIES variants. If the answer is no, skip.

Patterns are ordered 1–8 with the strongest, most-used patterns first. When two patterns tie on quality score, prefer the lower-numbered pattern.

## Pattern 1: Contrarian Refresh

**The move:** Name the boring default the entire category does. Invert it. Frame the inversion as the mechanism.

**Winning example (verbatim):**
> "I took Transparent Labs from $2m to $26m/yr in under 2 years using **humor to make their ads actually entertaining**"
> — Chamber Media / Template #1

**Reusable structural phrases:**
- "to make [thing] actually [adjective]"
- "[thing] that don't [boring default]"
- "by [doing the thing nobody else does]"

**Applicability question:** Is there a boring default in this category that the buyer is tired of? Did the agency's mechanism implicitly reject that default?

**When it fits hard:**
- The buyer's pain hierarchy includes exhaustion with the category's standard approach
- The literal mechanism contains something the category usually doesn't do (e.g., humor in supplement ads, customers as creatives, real influencers instead of polished UGC)
- The brief has a "fatigued by sameness" pain

**When it doesn't fit:**
- The mechanism IS the standard category move (e.g., "SEO ranking #1" — that's not contrarian, that's expected)
- The contrarian framing would be misleading about what the agency actually did

**v1 variant template:** "by [doing the contrarian thing] to make [outcome category] actually [contrarian adjective]"

## Pattern 2: Hidden Misattribution Fix

**The move:** Expose what the buyer THINKS they're optimizing. Reveal what they're ACTUALLY optimizing. Frame the gap-fix as the mechanism. The parenthetical is the whole point.

**Winning example (verbatim):**
> "Just helped Lightyear add $234K in ARR in 53 days without increasing ad spend - by **training target CPA models to focus on actual new customers (not just site visitors or form fills)**"
> — Tiger Tracks / Template #7

**Reusable structural phrases:**
- "focusing on [actual goal] (not just [thing they think is the goal])"
- "[doing the right thing] instead of [the thing they're accidentally doing]"
- "fixing the [hidden flaw] that's costing them [outcome]"

**Applicability question:** Is there a hidden misattribution in the buyer's current setup that the agency's mechanism specifically corrects?

**When it fits hard:**
- The buyer has a measurement / attribution / targeting flaw they don't know about
- The brief includes "we thought X but it's actually Y" patterns in transcripts
- The mechanism's value comes from REPLACING something the buyer is already doing wrong, not adding something new

**When it doesn't fit:**
- The mechanism is purely additive (they weren't doing anything wrong, you just did something new)
- There's no hidden flaw to expose — the buyer is fully aware of the problem already

**v1 variant template:** "by [fixing the actual thing] (not [the thing they assume they're doing])"

## Pattern 3: Constraint Negation ("Without X")

**The move:** State the outcome, then negate the thing the buyer assumes is required to get it. The "without X" clause does all the work.

**Winning examples (verbatim):**
> "I helped Dr Axe add $113K in monthly organic revenue in 90 days - mainly by ranking them first on Google for terms like {keyword} - **without spending on ads**"
> — Velox Media / Template #9

> "Now they're writing around 15-20 new commercial policies every month **without relying on referral networks or cold calling**"
> — Exchange Media / Template #10

> "Just helped Lightyear add $234K in ARR in 53 days **without increasing ad spend**"
> — Tiger Tracks / Template #7

**Reusable structural phrases:**
- "without [the thing the buyer assumes they have to do]"
- "without [spending / hiring / launching] [the thing]"
- "without [the painful old method]"

**Applicability question:** What does the buyer assume they have to do/spend/hire to get this outcome? Did the agency NOT do that thing?

**When it fits hard:**
- The buyer's pain hierarchy includes "we tried [X] and it didn't work" or "we can't afford to [X]"
- The mechanism explicitly avoids a costly/painful step the buyer assumes is mandatory
- Three highest-leverage X's for DTC: "without burning more ad spend", "without hiring an in-house creative team", "without launching new SKUs"
- Three highest-leverage X's for service businesses: "without cold calling", "without referrals", "without paying for [thing]"

**When it doesn't fit:**
- The agency DID do the thing the buyer assumes is mandatory (don't lie)
- There's no clear "thing the buyer assumes is required" — pain hierarchy doesn't surface one

**v1 variant template:** "[plain-English outcome verb] [headline number] without [the assumed-required thing]"

## Pattern 4: Trigger / Moment Frame

**The move:** Not WHAT we do — WHEN we do it. Anchor the mechanism to a precise buyer-natural trigger event.

**Winning example (verbatim):**
> "We **run ads at the exact moment when commercial properties have water damage**"
> — Target Market / Template #3

**Reusable structural phrases:**
- "at the exact moment when [trigger event]"
- "the day [trigger event] happens"
- "right when [specific signal] hits"

**Applicability question:** Does the mechanism's value come from timing — specifically, catching a buyer/customer at a discrete event? Can you name that event in 4–6 words?

**When it fits hard:**
- The mechanism relies on signal/intent/event data (recently-funded, just-hired, just-launched, just-experienced-X)
- The buyer's pain is "we're spending money on people not ready to buy"
- The event is sketchable in one image (water damage, IPO filing, new CMO hired)

**When it doesn't fit:**
- The mechanism is steady-state — no discrete trigger
- The trigger is fuzzy or abstract ("when buyers are ready" — too vague)

**v1 variant template:** "by [doing the thing] the moment [specific trigger event]"

## Pattern 5: Position Engineering

**The move:** Don't describe what you did (tactics, channels, processes). Describe the POSITION the work engineered them into.

**Winning example (verbatim):**
> "I helped Shield AI go from cold outreach to investors chasing them - mostly by **making their CEO the go-to voice for AI defense**"
> — Firecracker PR / Template #2

**Reusable structural phrases:**
- "by making [client/person] the [position word] for [category]"
- "by turning [client] into the [position] of [category]"
- "by positioning [client] as [position]"

**Position words that work:** "the go-to voice", "the default choice", "the only [thing]", "the first stop", "the category king", "the obvious answer"

**Applicability question:** Did the agency's work change WHO the client IS in the market — not just what numbers moved? Can you name the new position in 3–6 words?

**When it fits hard:**
- The mechanism produced category dominance, mindshare, or recognition
- The buyer's deep dream includes "be known as the X in our space"
- The work has a "before/after position" arc, not just "before/after number" arc

**When it doesn't fit:**
- The work is pure performance marketing (numbers moved, position didn't change)
- The position word would be a stretch — naming the client "the go-to" when they're #3 is dishonest

**v1 variant template:** "by making [client] the [position word] for [category they now own]"

## Pattern 6: Visual Theft / Inversion

**The move:** Sketchable image + value comes from the enemy's pocket. The mechanism turns the competitor into the source.

**Winning example (verbatim):**
> "We helped Cymbiotika grow Amazon revenue from $200k to $1.5m/year in 7 months - **using targeted ads to redirect competitors' traffic straight to their products**"
> — Pirawna / Template #11

**Reusable structural phrases:**
- "redirecting [competitor's resource] straight to [client]"
- "intercepting [competitor's customers] before they reach [competitor]"
- "stealing [competitor's signal] and using it for [client]"

**Applicability question:** Does the mechanism take something the competitor owns or generates and redirect it to the client? Is the image sketchable (arrows from competitor to client)?

**When it fits hard:**
- The mechanism uses competitor keywords, competitor audiences, competitor lookalikes, or competitor pricing
- The buyer is in a saturated market where competitors have the eyeballs/budget/share
- The visual works on a napkin

**When it doesn't fit:**
- The mechanism is competitor-agnostic
- There's no specific competitor signal being redirected — just generic targeting

**v1 variant template:** "by [redirect verb] [competitor's resource] straight to [client]"

## Pattern 7: Named Methodology Abstraction

**The move:** Name the umbrella concept in 2–3 words. Don't list tactics. The named thing has to be intriguing enough that the buyer asks "what's that?"

**Winning example (verbatim):**
> "We're booking Chamber Media meetings with CMOs at brands like AthleanX (spending $10m/yr on ads) using **signal-based outbound**"
> — Scaletopia / Template #13

**Reusable structural phrases:**
- "[adjective]-based [common noun]"
- "[verb]-driven [system]"
- "[methodology name] for [outcome]"

**Method-naming formula:** [Distinctive descriptor] + [recognizable category noun] = name. The descriptor must be specific enough to differentiate, the noun must be recognizable enough to not require explanation.

Examples that work: "signal-based outbound", "viral micro-influencer seeding", "intent-triggered ads", "founder-led PR"

Examples that don't work: "the [client] method", "proprietary framework", "AI-powered system" — too generic, no information

**Applicability question:** Can you compress this mechanism into 2–3 words that are (a) specific enough to be interesting and (b) recognizable enough not to need explanation?

**When it fits hard:**
- The mechanism has a named-in-the-industry term that's still novel to most buyers (seeding, intent data, lookalikes)
- The strategist wants a low-commitment teaser — full mechanism unfolds on the call
- The buyer reads "what's that?" not "huh?" when they see the named term

**When it doesn't fit:**
- The named version is generic to the point of meaning nothing
- The mechanism is too tactic-heavy to compress without losing the value

**v1 variant template:** "using [2–3 word named method]"

## Pattern 8: Plain-English Tactic (The Floor)

**The move:** Just say what the agency did, in plain English, in 5–15 words. No structural twist. Works when the tactic itself is universally recognized as desirable.

**Winning examples (verbatim):**
> "by **ranking them #1 locally for mental health treatments**"
> — Leadgenix / Template #4

> "by **reaching #1 on Google for 7 high-intent keywords**"
> — Velox / Template #8

> "by **ranking them first on Google for terms like {keyword}**"
> — Velox / Template #9

**Reusable structural phrases:**
- "by [doing X] for [specific thing]"
- "by [verb] [tactic] for [specific quantity / scope]"

**Applicability question:** Is the tactic universally recognized as a desirable outcome on its own (no reframing needed)? Examples: ranking #1 on Google, hitting a specific keyword, scaling to X creatives, doubling something specific.

**When it fits hard:**
- The tactic is well-known and the achievement is the point (SEO #1, scaling spend without CAC moving, hitting a Joe Rogan placement)
- The strategist needs a no-frills variant as a safety net
- The buyer is sophisticated enough to evaluate the tactic on its merits without theatrics

**When it doesn't fit:**
- The tactic requires explanation (then use Pattern 7: Named Methodology)
- The tactic is jargon-heavy in its plain form (then translate jargon, but if the translation removes all credibility, the tactic isn't ready for SMS)

**v1 variant template:** "by [verb] [tactic in plain English] [optional: specific quantity or scope]"

## Patterns NOT in this library

These are explicitly excluded because they do not appear in the 13 winning templates database. Do not invent variants using these:

- **Volume + unexpected source** ("1,000 unpaid micros") — appears in no winning SMS. The volume alone doesn't sell.
- **Hybrid system** ("X combined with Y") — appears in no winning SMS. Hybridizing reads as complex, not clever.
- **Flywheel / system noun** — "system" appears as a case-study connector in Template #12 but never as a mechanism descriptor in a winning example.
- **Compressed throughput** ("X per month / week") — appears in no winning SMS as a mechanism (only as an outcome stat — e.g., "15-20 policies per month").

If a literal mechanism only fits one of these excluded patterns, that's a signal the mechanism isn't yet SMS-ready and the strategist should either find a different anchor (a number, a contrarian word, a named methodology) or revisit the case study for a sharper extraction.

## Decision rule when patterns conflict

If a literal mechanism legitimately fits 5+ patterns, prioritize in this order based on the brief's top pain:

| Top pain in brief | Prefer pattern |
|---|---|
| Buyer tired of category sameness | Pattern 1 (Contrarian Refresh) |
| Buyer has hidden attribution / measurement flaw | Pattern 2 (Hidden Misattribution Fix) |
| Buyer assumes they need to spend / hire / launch | Pattern 3 (Constraint Negation) |
| Buyer wastes money on wrong-timing prospects | Pattern 4 (Trigger / Moment Frame) |
| Buyer wants category dominance / recognition | Pattern 5 (Position Engineering) |
| Buyer is in saturated competitive market | Pattern 6 (Visual Theft / Inversion) |
| Buyer is intrigued by novelty / wants to learn | Pattern 7 (Named Methodology) |
| Buyer is sophisticated and wants concrete proof | Pattern 8 (Plain-English Tactic) |

Always generate at least 1 Plain-English Tactic variant — it's the safety net.
Always generate the highest-leverage pattern for the top brief pain — that's the lead variant.
