# Proof framing — the moves for saying the result

The raw result is rarely the best *said* version of itself. The job is to hand the strategist a menu of
**defensible ways to frame the same real win**, so they pick the one that hits hardest for this persona.
Reframing a real number is strategy. Inventing one is fabrication — never do that. Every framing has to
survive a closer saying it on a sales call.

## The moves

### 1. Chunk up — extrapolate to a bigger, still-true frame
Scale the real result to a larger honest window or scope.
- per-quarter → per-year (×4): **"$1.5M in Q4" → "~$6M/yr"**
- per-client → portfolio/aggregate · per-month → annualized run-rate
- Rule: it has to be **defensible** — a run-rate or annualization the strategist could justify, not a
  fantasy. "We did $1.5M in their best quarter, that's a $6M/yr pace" is honest framing.

### 2. Chunk down — make it concrete and sharp
Trade a big abstract number for a vivid specific one.
- annual → a tight window: **"$6M/yr" → "$1.5M in 3 months"**
- a vague "grew revenue" → the exact figure · a blended number → the single sharpest one
- Use when specificity buys more belief than scale does.

### 3. Drop the weakening qualifier
Cut words that **discount** the win or **date** it.
- **"in Q4"** — implies the easy busy season did the work, AND goes stale the moment Q4 passes. Drop it
  or replace with a neutral timeframe ("in 3 months").
- "during a sale / promo", "one-off", "a one-time push" — all quietly hand the credit away.
- Keep only qualifiers that *add* belief (a real timeframe, a named constraint that's impressive).

### 4. Recast into the persona's native unit
Say the result in the currency *that buyer keeps score in* (this is the single highest-leverage move —
get it wrong and the whole text reads "not for me"):
- **Founder / CEO** → revenue / topline / "added $X"
- **Sales leader / CRO** → calls / demos / **shows** / pipeline / closed deals
- **Agency owner** → retainers / clients signed
- **Local / home services** → jobs / bookings / appointments
- **Ecom operator** → MER / ROAS / new-customer revenue / CAC
Same underlying win, different unit. ("84 qualified seats" → "85 qualified demos" for a CRO; → "14
retainers" for an agency owner.)

### 5. Re-time — pick the strongest *true* window
Sometimes a longer real window is *more* believable ("in under 2 years" reads solid, not hype);
sometimes a shorter one is punchier ("in 53 days"). Offer both where both are true.

### 6. Pick the strongest currency: topline vs profit vs efficiency
$ revenue, vs profit, vs MER/ROAS/CAC. Lead with what this persona cares about *and* what's strongest.
A 4.2x MER means more to an ecom operator than raw topline; a founder wants the revenue number.

### 7. Before→after vs single number
- **Transformation** ("$2m → $26m/yr") when the delta is dramatic — it shows the journey.
- **Single number** ("added $234K in 53 days") when the delta is less striking but the absolute is.

### 8. Status / authority framing — the proof with NO number
The strongest proof is often **not a metric** — it's a status fact that's un-arguable and un-dashboard-able.
**Mine the master-sheet "Other Notable Results" + the tier guide** for these (the S/A-tier "unique/rare
results" column literally lists them): a **#1 position** ("the #1 food brand on TikTok Shop"), **outranked a
big competitor** ("outranked Sephora & Ulta"), **press** (TechCrunch, WSJ, AGT), a **record broken**, an
**acquisition**, a famous **logo**. These need no %-conversion and no "is that run-rate defensible" — and
they often break a belief on their own ("a serious brand *is* on this channel"). Don't lead with GMV/vanity
when a status proof is sitting in the notable-results column.

### 9. Generate 2-3 outcome tiers — and lead with the STRONGEST, not the literal metric
Don't hand back one framing of the given number. Generate **2-3 distinct outcomes** at different levels and
**lead with the strongest**, which is frequently the status proof or a bolder reframe — not the raw headline
metric. E.g.: a **status** outcome (#1 / record), a **topline** outcome (the big $ in a tight window), and a
**bolder reframe** (a defensible run-rate / a "this year" total / an incremental-halo number). Offering
several outcome tiers is also how the strategist gets variants to A/B test. (Lead-with-strongest is a
judgment call — surface them ranked, let the strategist pick.)

## Worked example — Wufers (Acceler8, pet-food DTC, Meta+Amazon)

**Raw:** "$1.5M generated in Q4 · 4.2x MER in Q4 · 13M Amazon impressions · Q4 (3 months)."

**Bad (what wasn't ripping):** *"helped Wufers add $1.5m to their topline in Q4"*
- "in Q4" discounts it (peak gifting season did it) **and** it's stale now that Q4's long gone. Reads weird.

**Proof menu it should hand you:**
| Framing | Move | Fits |
|---|---|---|
| "$6M/yr in additional revenue" | chunk up (×4 run-rate) | founder — scale |
| "$1.5M in 3 months" | drop "Q4" + chunk down to the window | founder — concrete + recent-feeling |
| "$1.5M to their topline in their best quarter" | re-time, keep the win, lose "Q4" baggage | founder |
| "4.2x return on ad spend" | native unit (ecom) | ecom operator who lives in MER/ROAS |

→ The strategist picked **"$6M/yr"** (chunked up, dropped Q4). That's the move: give the menu, let
them choose how aggressive to be.

> Always end the proof menu by **asking how aggressive to chunk** — extrapolation is a judgment call the
> strategist owns (how defensible is the run-rate for *this* client?).
