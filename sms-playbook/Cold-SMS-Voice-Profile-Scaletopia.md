# COLD-SMS VOICE PROFILE: Scaletopia

*The tonal source-of-truth for Scaletopia cold SMS. Sits on top of the pipeline (sms-brief → mechanism-wordsmith → sms-draft) and governs which angle feels right, how the words land, the banlist, the casing, the CTA softness, and the final "would my closer say this?" gate. Built from a voice interview + the 17-text winners database, the M3 anatomy module, the GTM copy-review feedback docs, and the Master Sheet case-study tiering. Applied with judgment — never a cookie-cutter.*

---

## Core Identity

Cold SMS that **earns the bite** — the right person wanting to learn more — by landing like **one human texting one specific person, not a blast.** Proof grabs first (a recognizable logo + a transformation in the buyer's *own counting unit*, said exactly the way a closer would say it on a call), but proof only lands if it sounds **finger-typed**, disarms the "who is this?" guard-spike, and speaks the prospect's world. The merely-good text is believable; the best text feels **aimed**. The master sin is sounding **automated**; the master tiebreaker is **human over clever, every time.**

## SECTION 1: BELIEFS & CONTRARIAN TAKES ABOUT COLD OUTBOUND

### Q1: What do you believe about cold SMS you'd argue to 2am?
Social proof is close to mandatory — even with a unique service, proof is the number-one priority, and it has to be proof in the *metrics they actually want*. Proof grabs the bite (we've earned replies from 9-figure C-suite off the proof + logos alone). **But** it won't matter how good the proof is if it sounds like an automated text, or doesn't sound like you typed it with your fingers, or isn't in their tonality / their world. Familiarity + relevance is the gate; proof is the pull. — [OPERATOR TASTE]

### Q2: Is the human layer the gate, or the puller? + a real proof line.
Proof comes first and grabs attention. Then it's how you *humanize / slice and dice* it so it's specifically relevant and digestible to them. Real line, agency niche: *"We helped Chamber Media close 14 retainers in 5 months…"* — said to econ agencies who want DTC brands, naming household brands they'd want as clients. — [both]

### Q3: The native-unit belief and its edge.
Pick the metric in the currency that persona keeps score in. "14 retainers" beat "84 qualified seats" because *retainers* is how an agency owner counts wins. The bigger raw number ("84 qualified calls booked") only wins ~1 in 10 — for pipeline-minded buyers (head of sales, CRO) or operators who already run cold outreach and value it. You only know which by **testing**. — [OPERATOR TASTE]

### Q4: "The list is the message" — true, overrated, or cope?
Overrated-as-stated. List precision lowers the bar for copy and makes the right case study obvious (tight slice = e-comm agencies who talk ecom, ~2–3k TAM → easy to pick the resonant case). But copy stays load-bearing — *"if I had shittier copy it wouldn't work."* Loose lists get Clay/AI personalization; tiny TAMs get a hand-built custom list. — [OPERATOR TASTE]

### Q5: AI-written texts — where's the line?
AI doesn't write the copy. The human hand-writes the skeleton and the proof; Clay/AI fills **only** the bracketed relevance/ICP-signal slot at scale (varied by title/site/industry — "so you head growth / revenue / sales / biz dev"). The machine reverse-engineers the manual move; it never carries the voice. — [both]

### Q19: SMS vs email.
SMS is uncrowded — a prospect gets maybe 1–2 good pitches a *week* (vs a flood on email). The disarmers work *because* nobody runs cold SMS at volume yet; the same lines on email would be clocked instantly. So SMS gets to the point and can survive a bare case-study + CTA **if the case study is really, really good**; email needs more mechanism/substance. (Doc rule #14 — "bare case-study+CTA dies in sophisticated markets" — is an *email* rule.) — [OPERATOR TASTE]

### Q67/Q74: Levers, sophistication, and decay.
All levers work, even on sophisticated buyers — the choice depends on your *ammo* (case-study strength), not their sophistication. Jadedness is a top-10% concern, not a default. And copy **decays**: what worked 12 months ago won't work as well today — that's the nature of the game, so you innovate with small deviations, and the numbers crown new winners. — [OPERATOR TASTE]

### When NOT to use SMS.
SMS is the wrong channel for bottom-funnel offers that can't grab attention (e.g., website builds). Offer-dependent, not niche-dependent — no niche has proven uncrackable when the rules hold (accounting, insurance, legal all worked by leaning on the case study and sounding human). — [OPERATOR TASTE]

## SECTION 2: THE TEXT ITSELF — BITE MECHANICS

### Q35/Q36/M3: The anatomy of the pair.
Two texts; **T2 sends ~5 seconds after T1, same thread** (two texts read human; one long text reads like a spammy wall).

- **T1:** disarmer → identity → case study *(named logo + result in their unit + timeframe + [optional mechanism])*
- **T2:** relevance bridge to them → [optional authority stack / scarcity / curiosity hook] → soft CTA → [optional signature]

**Never-cut spine:** a disarmer (can be as light as "hey," or even just the signature) + identity (= signature) + the case study + relevance + a soft CTA.
**Optional / situational (cut when they don't fit — high-leverage when they do, not "low quality"):** mechanism, authority stack, scarcity, curiosity hook.
Winners don't break this anatomy — the anatomy *is* the pattern. T1 earns the right to T2. — [ON-THE-TEXT RULES]

### Q6/Q7/Q13/M3: The opener + disarmer.
The opener's job on SMS is to **look phone-native and lower the guard** (survive "who is this and why are they texting me?") — *not* to be clever or lead with heavy relevance (that spikes the guard on a stranger's text). "Had an idea" is a guard-lowering setup whose payoff is the relevance line later.

Disarmer is near-mandatory. Decision table (from the M3 module — it *is* systematized, not vibes):

| Context | Disarmer |
|---|---|
| Casual (DTC, creators, small agencies) | "bit random but…" |
| Professional (B2B services) | "I know this is random but…" |
| Sophisticated/skeptical (SaaS, agencies) | "feel free to ignore if…" (or skip if ultra-specific relevance) |
| Situation-based | "bit of a weird situation…" |
| Bold curiosity claim | "you don't know me but I think I cracked the code to [X]" |

Permission-disarmers ride a problem; humble-bold disarmers ride a claim. **Rotate the interrupt across follow-ups to the same prospect** (same pattern twice = "this is a pitch"). Freshly-minted interrupts are an edge. The disarm always comes from a **peer** position, never an apology. — [ON-THE-TEXT RULES]

### Q12/M3: Identity.
Identity = signature — the same thing, interchangeable: "it's [Name] from [Company]" at the top of T1, **or** "– Name, Company" at the bottom of T2. Placement is decided by character budget + opener boldness (a bold claim like "you don't know me but I cracked the code" *needs* the ID nearby or it raises alarms). Company name must be real; the sender's first name can be invented (a female name is mildly preferable). — [ON-THE-TEXT RULES]

### Q8/Q9: Numbers — the sales-call rule.
**The master test: if your closer would say it on a sales call, it works; if it sounds like a dashboard, it dies.** "Halved their CAC," not "cut CAC by 200%." "$10M profitably," not "$2.58M → $10.4M." Revenue, appointments, clients — what they care about. Granularity no human would text reads nerdy/automated ("saw your traffic was 87.2k," "page down 20%" = scraped/robotic). — [both]

### Q10/Q11/Q30/Q31: Mechanism *(note: the mechanism-wordsmith playbook owns this — kept light here on purpose).*
In when it's genuinely unique, the market wants it, it's tied to the result, and it's **payable on the call**. Abstraction is *licensed by case-study strength*: strong proof (transformation + logo) → you can fly high with a sticky label ("neurology marketing," "humor that actually entertains"); weaker/chunked proof → drop to a plain **picture** and explain a little. Describe the mechanism as a picture you can *see* ("gifting free product to 100 creators a month and running ads off their posts"), not a jargon **label** ("viral micro creator seeding strategy"). Always banned: the nerdy-literal ("by focusing on the amygdala") and the backend-technical ("enterprise domain infrastructure for 100% deliverability"). **Often omitted entirely** when the case study is strong + specific. — [both]

### Q7/Q8/Q49: Relevance at human altitude.
Relevance must be at the altitude a real person would notice and text — "just saw you hired Jack" (human) beats "saw your traffic was 87.2k" (scraped/nerdy). It's not about surveillance; on B2B it's about not sounding automated/nerdy. A genuine bridge = real research + a why-them-why-now ("saw your work with Y…"). "Saw [company], had a few ideas" is weak — it survives only on a killer case study. — [both]

### Q24: The "without X".
"Without X" = the buyer's **common objection or core pain, compressed** — "takes too long" → counter with the timeframe; "without founder dependency" for a solo/small operation. It must match their actual situation (say "without founder dependency" to a 100-person agency and it's irrelevant). Accurate research is the prerequisite. — [both]

### Q15/Q16/Q17/Q54: Length, casing, punctuation.
- **Length:** no hard char count — fit the SMS preview, look natural on a phone, and **balance the pair** (long T1 → short T2, and vice versa). Winners run ~190 chars T1 / ~150 T2 on average; ~290 is too long. — [ON-THE-TEXT]
- **Casing:** normal sentence case, brand keeps its caps; occasional lowercase to feel human; "u"/"lmk" fine casually, pull toward proper for sophisticated/legal niches. Don't over-engineer it. — [ON-THE-TEXT]
- **Punctuation/rhythm:** ~95% of CTAs end in "?"; ~1 in 20 a period ("lmk if I can give you a ring"). Vary sentence length (short/med/long) to break the AI flatline. — [ON-THE-TEXT]

### Q16/Q17/Q37/Q38: CTA.
Soft + human is the gate. Default **"could I show u how?"** (a soft-hybrid — a yes funnels to a call without asking for one). Old-school niches → "can I give you a quick ring?" Hard ceiling is "worth exploring?" / "worth a chat?" — **never "got 15 Thursday?"** The CTA points back to the **proof** ("how *we* did it"), never promises *their* outcome ("how *you* can cut CPC"). — [ON-THE-TEXT]

### Q38/Q51: Levers (the spread).
Same case study, sliced by which *detail* T2 features:
- **Unique / curiosity** → feature the unusual mechanism detail ("1200 unpaid micro creators") → "want to learn how."
- **FOMO / authority** → feature the logo stack ("did this for Gruns & Create Wellness too") → implies everyone-but-you.
- **Timely** → the ICP signal / right-time trigger (hiring, news, algo update, recent CMO) + a why-now.
- **Helpful** → give value (estimate / audit / strategy) *without* asking for business — needs an authority base under it.
Levers hybridize; the case study is the spine you flex around. CTA stays soft and stable. — [both]

### Q40: Variation across a list.
SMS deliverability is forgiving vs email, so identical sends aren't a paranoia — name + company merge gives baseline uniqueness. Run 2–3 variants (V1/V2/V3), find the winner, and **ride it**. The Clay ICP-signal variable is the highest performer because it's unique to each person. ("Never repeat across a list" is an email worry.) — [OPERATOR TASTE]

## SECTION 3: TONAL CRIMES (THE BANLIST)

*Pulled from the GTM copy-review docs, the winners audit, and the operator's own kills. Not a fixed word list — a **smell**: AI slop + writing to everyone instead of one specific person. The test: would you say this out loud, in a text, to one specific person?* **(Flagged for sign-off — see end.)**

### Opener crimes
- "Sorry to text" / "sorry to bother you" / "sorry to waste your time" — apologetic, reads like a chump, speaks *beneath* them. ("Sorry for the random" is fine.) — [ON-THE-TEXT]
- "I understand if your initial reaction is to pass on this, but…" / "I totally understand if you get 50 of these but…" — negative previewing; positions you as email #51 before you've said anything. — [ON-THE-TEXT]
- Poke-the-bear / rhetorical openers ("even a 3x ROAS can cost you money," "every plumbing co bids on the same keywords") — friction before they've engaged; almost never works in SMS. — [OPERATOR TASTE]
- Fake leading questions you could answer yourself ("is ChatGPT naming [competitor] instead of you?", "want me to show you where you stand?") — manufactured curiosity, reads AI. — [both]
- Claim-before-context ("your engineering team's salaries qualify for R&D credits…") — reads like a scam text. Who/why must land before the claim; the first 5–7 words decide read-or-bin. — [both]

### AI / robot tells
- Percentages and decimals (**the single biggest concrete ban — convert to absolutes**), vague metrics ("doubled," "significantly improved"). — [ON-THE-TEXT]
- "It's not just X — it's Y" / "X, not Y" (the AI contrast move). — [ON-THE-TEXT]
- Triadic lists ("streamline operations, scale efficiently, and unlock growth"); adjective-stacking ("strategic, scalable, and sustainable"). — [ON-THE-TEXT]
- Empty verbs: leverage, unlock, empower, drive, streamline, optimise, robust, seamless, pivotal, crucial. Corporate jargon real people don't use (solution, proprietary, world-class, best-in-class, synergy). — [ON-THE-TEXT]
- Too-perfect grammar throughout (real humans drop articles, lowercase, fragment); the two-sentence-same-energy flatline ("4x'd their demos. We did this for Awardco."); the "podium voice" (reads mid-presentation, not texting). — [ON-THE-TEXT]
- "+" notation ("$35k+", "140+") → use "$35k", "140". Category labels in parens ("(DTC apparel)"). Redundant tool names ("Klaviyo/Postscript" → "Klaviyo"). Vague "model"/"system" with no name. "Moved your number" (a no-one-says-this-out-loud buzzword). — [ON-THE-TEXT]
- **Brag-superlatives / AI flourishes:** "the biggest month in their history," "their best month ever," "record-breaking," "of all time," "fastest-growing ever." A closer **states the number** ("$800k to $4m in a month") and stops — the superlative tail is the AI tell. Say the number; drop the flourish. — [ON-THE-TEXT]

### Relevance / structure crimes
- Slapping on a case study with no context; random numbers with no connection to anything; too many stats crammed in. — [both]
- Leading with "we" / we-heavy ("we've been testing," "we created a model"). — [both]
- No relevance to them specifically; a pain point that isn't actually their priority; an offer that doesn't connect to what they want. — [OPERATOR TASTE]
- Mass-announcement feel instead of a one-to-one text. — [both]
- **The statement-T2 / market thesis** — a T2 that *declares what their category does* ("most [niche] brands are still all-in on X," "[niche] brands can't scale creative fast enough") instead of speaking **to them**. The category-noun as the subject of a state verb (are/can't/keep/still) = a LinkedIn-post line, not a text. Fix: make it a first-person observation or a line about *them* ("haven't seen many do this yet — could I show u how it'd work for you?"). — [both]

### CTA crimes
- "could I drop you an email," "jump on a call," "book a 30-min strategy session," "would you be open to…," "let me know your thoughts." — [ON-THE-TEXT]
- Salesy outcome-CTAs ("can I show you how *you* can reduce CPC / get more clients"); AI-CTAs ("Should I share how we can set that up?"). — [ON-THE-TEXT]

## SECTION 4: VOICE & PERSONA

### Q47/Q60: The character.
A **distant peer who's in their industry** — almost one of them. Exec-to-exec for execs; "basically another plumber" for a plumber. American. Talks their shop, at their level. **Not** a personal friend (a friend wouldn't know their world), so **no small talk, no warm-up, straight to the point** — but warm, not robotic. — [OPERATOR TASTE]

### Q33/Q34: Confidence vs neediness.
Confidence is **earned** — you write for 7–8-figure clients who genuinely can help. The voice sits **on their level or just above, never beneath** ("sorry to bug you / I'll keep this quick" = beneath = dead, the same way you'd never open a sales call apologizing for your existence) and **never arrogant** ("I scaled X, want to be next?"). Soft hedges about *fit* are fine ("thought it could be relevant," "might work for you"). — [both]
**Peer vs SDR:** the SDR over-explains, gets too technical/nuanced, sounds timid, and always pushes for the call. The peer speaks from inside their world, stays soft, and is eliciting a *response* — not chasing a meeting. — [both]

### Q39: Humor.
None in the copy — no jokes, no "lol." It's one-way, cold, B2B; humor needs a two-way familiarity you don't have. Casual ≠ comedic: stay **casual but straight-faced**. The only "humor" is the 😂 on the "weird approach" disarm. — [both]

### Q22/Q23: Emoji + the disarm.
😂 only, and only when the approach is genuinely a bit weird/funny, to drop the guard. Never a self-impressed wink (e.g., "…Eagles and Yankees as clients 😉 — yes, you read that right" = automatic cringe). — [ON-THE-TEXT]

### Q32/Q48: Soul + rhythm.
Soul = **a human recognizing another human** — does it feel like someone actually texted *this*, to *you*? Soul is the content layer (specificity, the levers actually firing, recency); rhythm is the structure layer (vary short/med/long; AI flatlines with even pacing, one long compound sentence, every beat the same weight, grammar too clean). The best copy has both. — [both]

### Q72: "I" vs "we".
Both are fine. "I" sounds more personal / one-to-one (preferred for the intimate feel); "we" sounds a bit more commercial (fine for bigger companies, and for "we helped [client] [result]"). Bad-we = about-us ("we've been testing," "we created a model"). Mix per context. — [ON-THE-TEXT]

### Q34: Reply handling *(adjacent to the copy).*
"Stop texting me" → don't respond, put them on DND. "Who is this / how'd you get my number?" → honest + casual + an out: *"it's Armin from Scaletopia, got a tool that scrapes LinkedIn, thought I'd give you a shout — if it's not relevant, lmk and I won't text again."* The funnel logic: acknowledge it's cold → drop the guard → give the result → get them **off the text onto a call fast.** The text moves to a call; it doesn't converse. — [OPERATOR TASTE]

## SECTION 5: STRUCTURE & SEQUENCE

### Q20/Q21/M3: Division of labor + flow.
T1 = disarmer + identity + proof; T2 = relevance bridge + soft CTA (+ optional authority stack / signature). **T2 must flow out of T1, not sit beside it.** T2's non-negotiable = relevance-to-them + a CTA — never a brag with a limp CTA. The split is fluid (identity/proof can slide between T1 and T2). One clean idea per text. — [ON-THE-TEXT]

### Q21: Follow-ups.
Follow-ups aren't the engine — the pair carries it, and over-texting reads spammy. The one follow-up that works is a **risk-reversal / guarantee / pilot flip** ("10 clients in 30 days or you don't pay" / "we run a 30-day pilot — worth doing?"). That's also when a third text earns its place. — [OPERATOR TASTE]

## SECTION 6: HARD NOS

### Q25/Q26: Scarcity — manufacturable.
Scarcity can be manufactured — it's **not** a hard no. The gate is believability + niche-exclusivity FOMO: "we only run 18 brands at a time and have room for 1–2 more health & wellness brands" works because 18 is plausible and the niche-limit makes them think "who else are you running in my space?" — [OPERATOR TASTE]

### Q26/Q27/Q28: Numbers — the bright line.
You can reconstruct missing-attribution numbers with sensible math, and let timeframe/transformation drift a few months ("to elicit a response; nobody jumps on a call and says 'you're wrong'"). Non-round reads real ("14," not "25"). **HARD NO: a fully fake case study or invented client.** The real crime is sounding *off* — numbers that betray you don't know their world. Company name must be real; sender first name can be invented. — [both]

### Q37/Q38: The trap opener + salesy/fake CTAs.
**Never** a rhetorical / poke-the-bear question at the start (the capacity question — "can you take on more clients right now?" — only works at low sophistication, and even then it's lukewarm). Never a salesy outcome-CTA, never a fake leading question. Don't make claims about publicly-checkable things you haven't actually researched. — [both]

## SECTION 7: RED FLAGS (WHY I'D KILL A DRAFT INSTANTLY)

### Q31/Q32: The master filter + the three kills.
**Master filter: "Will this elicit a response from the prospect?"** Everything else is judgment in service of that.

1. **It doesn't sound human** — reads like a nerd, AI, or a mass text, not thumbed out for them. (Bulk / wall-of-text is how it shows up; the *phone-paste glance test* is how you catch it — paste it into Messages and see if a human would thumb it out.)
2. **No relevance** — not specifically about them; the "apologising-but-bragging" silhouette (a credibility dump where a relevant hook belongs).
3. **It doesn't speak their world** — wrong outcome unit ("calls" to a plumber who counts jobs/estimates; topline to someone who counts MRR); research too shallow to know what they're sick of hearing.

— [both]

### Q70: Best vs merely-good — the one extra thing.
The merely-good text clears the bar: believable, clear, a real person could've sent it. The **best** feels **aimed, not blasted** — like one specific person looked at one specific *you*, not a template with a name merged in. The lever is **relevance, dialed to how narrow the segment is** (the "for-me" bar). Merely-good copy reaches for *more proof* (another case study, a bigger number) — but proof only adds to "this is real," which is already handled, and does nothing for "you see me." Close partner: an unusual-but-believable hook (the "tell me more"). But if forced to one, it's aimed-ness. — [OPERATOR TASTE]

### Q65: The subtle good-operator miss.
A good operator can have the tone, structure, and copy down and still miss on **writer↔reader cognitive misalignment** — they weren't truly thinking about what elicits *this* reader's reply (often because the strategist hasn't met the ICP). The copy looks good on paper but the chunk/outcome is wrong. The fix is research: onboarding form + who they actually work with + **watching the sales-call transcripts**, compiled. — [OPERATOR TASTE]

## QUICK REFERENCE CARD

### Always (on the text)
- **[HARD RULE]** Sound like one human texting one specific person — finger-typed, not a blast.
- **[HARD RULE]** Lead with a disarmer that drops the guard; survive "who is this & why are they texting me?"
- **[HARD RULE]** State identity (top of T1 or as a "– Name, Company" signature).
- **[STRONG TENDENCY]** Proof = named logo + result in their *native unit* + timeframe, said like a closer on a call.
- **[STRONG TENDENCY]** Two texts, T2 ~5s after T1, same thread; balance their length (long T1 → short T2).
- **[STRONG TENDENCY]** Soft CTA that points to the proof ("could I show u how?"); end on a "?".
- **[STRONG TENDENCY]** Relevance at human altitude + a why-them-why-now.
- **[STRONG TENDENCY]** Prefer recency where there's no big transformation ("last month…").
- **[LIGHT PREFERENCE]** "I" over "we" for the one-to-one feel; lowercase touches to feel human.

### Never (the banlist)
- **[HARD RULE]** Sound like an automated blast text. *(The #1 sin — everything ladders to it.)*
- **[HARD RULE]** Percentages or decimals (use absolutes); vague metrics ("doubled," "significantly improved").
- **[HARD RULE]** Apologetic openers ("sorry to text"); negative previewing ("understand if you pass"); poke-the-bear / fake leading questions at the start.
- **[HARD RULE]** A fully fake case study or invented client.
- **[HARD RULE]** Salesy outcome-CTAs ("how *you* can cut CPC"); "drop you an email," "jump on a call," "30-min strategy session," "would you be open to."
- **[STRONG TENDENCY]** "It's not just X — it's Y" / "X, not Y"; triadic lists; adjective-stacking; empty verbs (leverage/unlock/empower/drive/streamline/optimise); corporate jargon.
- **[STRONG TENDENCY]** "+" notation; "(category label)" parens; redundant tool names; unnamed "model/system"; "moved your number."
- **[STRONG TENDENCY]** Leading with "we" / we-heavy; cramming too many stats; clever over human.
- **[HARD RULE]** Brag-superlatives ("biggest in their history," "best month ever," "record-breaking," "of all time") — state the number, drop the flourish.
- **[HARD RULE]** The statement-T2 / market thesis ("most [niche] brands are still…," "[niche] brands can't…") — speak *to them*, not about their category.
- **[HARD RULE]** Jokes / "lol" / a self-impressed wink 😉. (😂 only, on the weird-approach disarm.)

### Signature Openers (verbatim from winners)
- "Hi [name], it's Courtney from Chamber. I know this is random but…"
- "Hi [name], feel free to ignore if you've [already solved X], but…"
- "Hi [name], it's April from Target Market - bit random but…"
- "Hey [name], Steve from Stratedia. Know this is random but…"
- "Hi [name], it's Christy over at Velox Media - this might sound random, but hear me out."
- "Hi [name], not sure if you're at capacity, but…"
- "Hey [name], it's Courtney from Kynship, you don't know me but I think I cracked the code to [outcome]."
- "appreciate this is outta nowhere but hear me out…"
- *(rotate these; never stamp one across a whole list — the signature is a flavor, not a stamp.)*

### Signature CTAs (tagged)
- **Soft (default):** "could I show u how?" · "interested to see how it works?" · "worth a look?"
- **Soft w/ time anchor:** "lmk if it's worth exploring sometime next week" · "can we chat sometime tomorrow?"
- **Old-school niche:** "can I give you a quick ring?"
- **Hard ceiling (rare):** "worth a chat?" / "worth exploring?" — *no harder than this.*
- **Value-give (helpful):** "happy to share what's working in [X] right now, if useful?"

### Mechanism Voice *(light — defer to the mechanism-wordsmith playbook)*
Picture over label — verbs you can see ("gifting free product to 100 creators and running ads off their posts") beat jargon ("viral micro creator seeding strategy"). Strong proof licenses a sticky label ("neurology marketing"); weak proof needs the plain picture. Must be payable on the call. Omit it entirely when the case study is strong + specific.

### Per-Context Shifts
- **Plumber / home services:** super snappy, zero jargon, chunk *down* + recency ("last month we got them 54 jobs"); "give you a ring" works. Text early-day is fine.
- **Ecom founder:** the most-pitched — lead with hard authority: specific revenue + logos they know. Chunk *up* (scale + revenue + timeframe). No cold-call CTA.
- **Agency owner:** looser, peer-to-peer (one operator to another); you can get away with more casual.
- **SaaS / VC-backed:** mind the ego — don't be too informal; "feel free to ignore if…".
- **Legal (criminal defense etc.):** sophisticated, high-ego; proper casing; anchor credibility early; **text later in the day** (they're in court AM).
- *(Don't build a rigid per-niche voice — it's the same rules + "understand their world, stay on their level.")*

- "Don't sound like an automated blast text — that's the one I'd ban forever."

### Character & Rhythm Defaults
- **Length:** no hard cap; fit the preview, look natural on a phone; ~190 T1 / ~150 T2; ~290 = too long; balance the pair.
- **Numbers:** one strong number does more than two; absolutes, rounded, non-round reads real; never %.
- **Punctuation:** "?" closer ~95%; plain hyphen " - " for connective beats (never em-dash —, don't stack 3+); occasional lowercase; no semicolons, no curly quotes.
- **Rhythm:** mix short/med/long; break uniformity; no two-sentence-same-energy flatline.

## HOW TO USE THIS DOCUMENT (ANTI-OVERFITTING GUIDE)

This captures the taste — it is **not** a template to stamp onto every text.

### Spirit over letter
Internalise the sensibility. A text that uses 3 of these tendencies naturally beats one that forces in 10. The goal is a text that *earns the bite*, not one that checks boxes.

### Frequency labels
- **HARD RULE** — never violate (the Hard Nos and the core banlist).
- **STRONG TENDENCY** — do this 70–80% of the time; breaking it occasionally is correct.
- **LIGHT PREFERENCE** — context decides. (Assume LIGHT PREFERENCE when unlabeled.)

### Deliverability still wants variation — but SMS is forgiving
Don't ship 500 identical texts — but unlike email, SMS deliverability is forgiving, so a proven winner can be ridden. Name + company merge gives baseline uniqueness; rotate openers/interrupts; the Clay ICP-signal variable is the real per-person differentiator and the top performer. The signature opener is a *flavor*, not a stamp.

### Context matters
Voice flexes by persona (Founder/CEO ≠ Marketing leader ≠ plumber), by niche, and by **altitude** (household-name proof → fly high and casual; ordinary proof → fly low and let the specifics carry). Apply the Per-Context notes; don't flatten them. Don't over-engineer per-niche voice — it's the same rules + "understand their world, stay on their level."

### Don't over-correct into forced-casual
The winners are already clean of word-level AI tells. Protect that — but forcing "human-ness" (fake lowercase, try-hard casual) is its own tell. Casual ≠ comedic; casual ≠ sloppy.

### The litmus test (two prongs)
Before any text ships "as Scaletopia," ask:
> 1. Would my closer say this on a call without sounding rehearsed? *(governs the case study + mechanism)*
> 2. Read out loud / pictured arriving on their phone — does it sound like a smooth, one-to-one text that survives the "who is this?" guard-spike, or congested/nerdy/mass?

If it feels written, pull back. Less imitation, more inhabitation.

### What matters most
If you forget everything else:
1. **Earn the bite by sounding like one human texting one specific person** — proof pulls, but human + relevance is the gate.
2. **The signature pattern:** a self-aware, peer disarmer that drops the guard + proof in their native unit + a soft "could I show u how?" — finger-typed throughout.
3. **Never sound like an automated blast text** — and never choose clever over human.
